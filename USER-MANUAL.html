1What it is

The EVtivity EV Tester emulates the electric-vehicle side of a J1772 AC charging session. You plug it into a charging station (EVSE) in place of a car and drive the session through three states, so you can verify that the station advertises the right status, offers the right current, and handles connect, charge, suspend, unplug, and fault conditions - and that your CSMS logs it all.

It is a signal-level bench tool. It switches the J1772 Control Pilot state resistors and reads the pilot PWM. It never carries mains power; only the CP, PP, and GND signal lines reach the board. Use it for EVSE bring-up, OCPP conformance checks, and demos.

Power-up: what you see and hear

Every power-up runs the same sequence. It is worth knowing, because the sounds tell you what the tester is doing without looking at it.

WhenWhat happens
immediatelyBacklight on. The screen reads EVtivity Tester / AC J1772 v0.1.22.
~0.3-1.5 sThe startup fanfare - six rising notes, the "Charge!" motif - plays while the splash is up.
~1.5-2.0 sThe on-board LED blinks three times, then the tester self-checks its display connection.
~2.1 sSplash clears to the Ready screen. The tester is fully usable from here - buttons, pilot and fault injection all work, with or without WiFi.
~4.3 sIf WiFi is configured, it joins in the background. On success you hear a short rising two-note chime and the screen shows the web-panel IP address for four seconds.

With no WiFi configured, nothing further happens and the tester simply runs offline - that is normal, not a fault. If a configured network cannot be joined, it retries five times ten seconds apart, then powers the radio down and the Ready line reads WiFi failed; the tester keeps working throughout.

You should hear the fanfare exactly once per power-up. If it plays twice in a row, something reset the board a few seconds in. That is almost always a program on your computer opening the tester's USB serial port - see section 10.

Both 5-second holds reboot the tester, so each one is followed by the beep, then a short pause, then the full startup fanfare again. Hearing the fanfare after holding a button is expected and means the gesture worked. Hold MODE 5 s to enter setup, then any button 5 s to come back out.

4The three modes

Press MODE to cycle. There is no auto-advance; each press is deliberate.

Tester showsJ1772 stateOCPP 1.6OCPP 2.1 (connector / charging)
ReadyState AAvailableAvailable / Idle
PreparingState BPreparingOccupied / EVConnected
ChargingState CChargingOccupied / Charging
SuspendedState BSuspendedEVOccupied / SuspendedEV
  • Starting Charging needs a live station. With no pilot present, selecting Charging is refused with No charger / Plug in first and an error tone; the mode does not change.
  • Charging is a two-step handshake. It first presents State B (so the station starts its pilot PWM), waits ~1 s, then switches to State C to draw. The label reads Preparing until the station offers current, then Charging.
  • Unplugging resets to Ready. If the pilot disappears while Charging or Suspended, the tester returns to Ready on its own.

6Fault injection

To force a pilot fault on the charger, hold both buttons during an active Charging session. The tester collapses the positive half of the Control Pilot, which a compliant station reads as a pilot fault (J1772 State E) and responds to by opening its contactor and stopping the session safely; the LCD shows * FAULT INJECT * / pilot collapsed. Release both buttons to recover.

Whether the charger then reports OCPP Faulted to your CSMS is up to its firmware, so do not assume it will. Many stations classify a Control Pilot anomaly as an EV-side communication error rather than an internal fault: they stop the session but send a StatusNotification with a status such as SuspendedEV or Finishing and an errorCode of EVCommunicationError, not status Faulted. A true OCPP Faulted usually requires a station-internal fault (ground fault, over-temperature, a failed relay), which a vehicle-side tester cannot produce. Check both the status and errorCode fields at the CSMS to see how your charger classified it.

Fault injection only does something while the charger is actively charging. In Ready or Preparing the gesture is refused: with a charger present but not yet charging the LCD shows Charge first / to inject fault, and with no charger connected it shows No charger.

7WiFi setup

WiFi is optional - the tester is fully functional without it. A unit with no saved network simply runs offline with the radio off.

Enter setup mode

Hold MODE for 5 seconds (or, on the web panel, hold the on-screen MODE button 5 s). The tester reboots into its setup access point:

  • Network: EVtivity-EV-Tester-XXXX (open, no password). XXXX is a 4-character suffix unique to each unit (from its chip ID), so several testers on one bench stay distinct - the same name shows in your WiFi list and the router's client list.
  • Address: http://192.168.4.1 (a setup page usually opens automatically)

Enter your WiFi name and password and save; the tester restarts and joins. You can also set the web login and MQTT here. To leave setup without changing anything, hold any button 5 s (or power-cycle).

If saved credentials fail, the Ready line shows WiFi failed and the radio keeps retrying quietly - the tester keeps working. There is no automatic AP fallback; enter setup deliberately with the 5 s hold.

8Web control panel

Once on your network, browse to the tester's IP (shown on the Ready line). The panel:

  • Mirrors the LCD live on a virtual 16x2 display.
  • Has on-screen MODE and STATS buttons that act exactly like the physical ones - including hold MODE 5 s to enter WiFi setup.
  • Shows the mode-to-OCPP status table, an MQTT info panel (section 9), and a Fun page.

The panel is login-protected (default admin / admin). WiFi, login, and MQTT settings can only be changed in setup mode - the connected panel has no settings form, so a live tester can't be reconfigured or locked out by accident.

9MQTT remote control

Configure a broker in setup mode (leave the broker blank to keep MQTT off). Each device defaults to its own base topic, evtester-XXXX - the same 4-character suffix as its setup-AP name (shown on the web panel's MQTT button) - so several testers on one broker stay independent. Change it in setup mode if you want a specific name. The tester connects to your broker as a client and:

A broker is a separate small server that both the tester and your control tools connect to - it is not the tester's own IP address. If you don't already run one, see Running a local broker below.

Mode commands

Publish a one-word payload to <topic>/mode:

PayloadEffect
readyGo to Ready (State A).
charge (also charging preparing)Start Charging - needs a live charger, same guard as the button.
suspend (also suspended)Suspend (State B).
faultInject a pilot fault - needs a live charger; any other mode command clears it.

Song commands

Publish a one-word payload to <topic>/song:

PayloadEffect
hbdPlay Happy Birthday.
xmasPlay Merry Christmas.
canonPlay Canon in D.
stopStop the song.

Publishes state

  • <topic>/state - retained: ready / charging / suspended / faulted, updated on every change.
  • <topic>/availability - online, with offline set as the broker's last-will so a dropped tester shows offline.

Examples (replace evtester-XXXX with your device's topic): mosquitto_pub -h BROKER -t evtester-XXXX/mode -m charge (mode) and mosquitto_pub -h BROKER -t evtester-XXXX/song -m hbd (song). The web panel's MQTT button shows the live broker, connection state, and these topics filled in with the configured device's values.

Running a local broker

MQTT needs a broker - a small server the tester and your control tools all connect to. For bench use the simplest broker is Mosquitto running on your own laptop or an always-on host on the same network. Install it (macOS shown; Linux apt install mosquitto, Windows from mosquitto.org):

brew install mosquitto

Mosquitto 2.x listens on localhost only and rejects anonymous clients by default, so the tester on the LAN can't reach a bare install. Save this two-line config (for example mosquitto-lan.conf) to open it to the LAN:

listener 1883 0.0.0.0
allow_anonymous true

Start the broker with that config:

mosquitto -c mosquitto-lan.conf -v

Find the host's LAN IP (macOS: ipconfig getifaddr en0). Then in the tester's setup mode set the MQTT broker to that IP and port 1883, with username and password blank; leave the topic at its per-device default. Save; the tester reboots onto your WiFi and connects.

  • The tester, the broker host, and any control tool must be on the same network.
  • If the tester won't connect, check the broker host's firewall allows inbound TCP 1883.
  • The host's IP can change on DHCP renewal. If it stops connecting later, re-check the IP or reserve it on your router.
  • Anonymous LAN access is fine for a bench broker. Do not expose it to the internet.

The repo ships a ready-to-use console client, mqtt-test.py. Run python3 mqtt-test.py, point it at the same broker and topic, and scroll the mode/song commands with the arrow keys - no need to type mosquitto_pub lines by hand.

10Antenna & troubleshooting

The XIAO ESP32-S3 has no onboard antenna; the external U.FL antenna is mandatory for WiFi. If any network feature misbehaves, seat the antenna first.

SymptomMeaning / fix
Web panel / setup AP not visible, or WiFi never joinsThe radio can't transmit without the antenna. Clip the U.FL antenna firmly onto the connector by the USB port and reboot.
Setup AP visible but "connection failed", then vanishesForget the EVtivity-EV-Tester-XXXX network on the phone and rejoin - a stale cached association blocks the reconnect.
LCD says WiFi failedSaved credentials are wrong or the network is unreachable. Hold MODE 5 s to reconfigure. The tester still works.
No charger / Plug in firstYou tried to Charge (or a button action) with no pilot present. Plug into a live station first.
Offered: -- while ChargingThe station has not offered current yet, or isn't driving pilot PWM.
It dropped to Ready on its ownThe pilot was lost (a real or momentary unplug).
Splash and fanfare play twice every time you plug into a computerA program on the computer is opening the tester's USB serial port, and on this chip that resets it in hardware. Slicers and printer hosts are the usual culprits - UltiMaker Cura's "USB Printing" plugin scans every serial device looking for a printer. Turn it off in Cura: Marketplace → Installed → untick USB Printing, then restart Cura. Slicing is unaffected. OctoPrint, Pronterface and some IDEs do the same. On a wall adapter or barrel jack with no computer attached, this cannot happen.

12Safety

  • This is a signal-level test tool. Never connect mains (L1 / L2 / N) to the board; only CP, PP, and GND belong on J1.
  • Power the barrel jack from a 5 V supply only. The onboard protection cuts off a mis-plugged higher-voltage supply, but do not rely on it as a habit.
  • Use it only with equipment you are authorized to test.
